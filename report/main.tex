\begin{abstract}
We compare eight classical configurations—logistic regression, $k$-NN, random forest and RBF-SVM on flattened pixels and HOG—with a small convolutional neural network on Fashion-MNIST. The CNN achieved the highest held-out performance (accuracy 0.9274; macro-F1 0.9269), followed by the pixel RBF-SVM (0.8953; 0.8951). The HOG parameter study selected 6,084-dimensional features using validation data only. The official test set was evaluated once after model selection. The comparison has important limitations: the HOG SVM used a runtime-gated subset of 11,770 training images, the pixel logistic-regression fit emitted a convergence warning at 500 iterations, and the single-pass inference timings are preliminary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Problem Statement}
\label{sec:intro}
This project is Phase~1 (machine-learning evaluation only) of an inventory image tagging system.
The question is whether a small convolutional neural network (CNN), which learns spatial features, recognises similar
clothing classes better than classical models on fixed representations. The classical models are logistic
regression, $k$-NN, random forest and an RBF-kernel SVM; the fixed representations are flattened pixels and
histogram-of-oriented-gradients (HOG) features. The outcome is open: we report what we measure. The final
comparison covers nine models (four classical models on two representations, plus one CNN) on Fashion-MNIST.

\section{Methodology and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:method}
We use the official split only. The 60{,}000 official training images are divided into a stratified
54{,}000-image training split and a 6{,}000-image validation split with seed 42; the 10{,}000 official test
images are used for evaluation only once, at the final test gate (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}); before the gate they are read only for loading and duplicate checks, and no test statistic enters any fitting or selection step. Nothing (scalers, mean and
standard deviation, or any other fitted preprocessing) is fitted on validation or test data. Pixel features are
scaled to $[0,1]$. Logistic regression and the SVM use a standardisation step inside the pipeline; $k$-NN and
random forest use no scaler; the CNN uses the mean and standard deviation of the training split. Final models are
trained on the 54{,}000-image training split only, with no refit on training plus validation.

\section{Experimental Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:protocol}
Model selection uses the highest validation macro-F1, with validation accuracy breaking ties; the SVM is tuned
on a stratified 10{,}000-image subset of the training split. Before every final classical fit the fit time is
estimated, and if it exceeds the limit the model is fitted on a disclosed stratified subset; the number of
training images used is always reported. After selection the chosen configurations are written to a freeze file
and all nine models are evaluated on the test images exactly once. 

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Accuracy is the fraction of correctly classified images. For class $c$, $F1_c = 2TP_c/(2TP_c+FP_c+FN_c)$, and
macro-F1 is the unweighted mean of the ten $F1_c$ values, so every class counts equally. The test set has exactly
1{,}000 images per class, so macro-averaged recall equals accuracy; macro-F1 differs because it also depends on
precision. Confusion matrices have the true class in rows and the predicted class in columns.

\paragraph{Uncertainty and paired comparison.}
We report 95\% percentile bootstrap confidence intervals~\cite{efron1979bootstrap}: the 10{,}000 test images are
resampled with replacement 1000 times (seed 42). These intervals capture the sampling noise of this test set for one
trained model; they do not include the randomness of training. To compare the CNN with each classical model on the
same images we use McNemar's test~\cite{mcnemar1947note} on the paired correctness of the two models (exact binomial
test when there are fewer than 25 discordant pairs, otherwise a chi-square statistic with continuity correction), with a
Bonferroni correction for the eight comparisons. This test also ignores training variance~\cite{dietterich1998approximate}.

\paragraph{Inference cost.}
Timing uses the same 10{,}000 test images in a fixed order, and excludes model loading, training and plotting. For the
classical models we report ``predict only'' (precomputed features) and ``end-to-end'' (feature extraction from the raw
images, then prediction) separately, as one timed pass after an untimed warm-up call in the saved run. These single-repeat values are preliminary, not stable latency estimates. For the CNN the inputs
are pre-normalised tensors, batch size 256, with three warm-up batches; the recorded table contains a single timing per device. CNN tensor timing excludes image preprocessing and is not directly comparable to classical end-to-end timing. The notebook records the library versions and the hardware it ran on
(\texttt{results/environment.json}); implementations are scikit-learn~\cite{pedregosa2011scikit},
PyTorch~\cite{paszke2019pytorch} and scikit-image~\cite{vanderwalt2014scikit}.

\section{Confusion Matrices and Error Analysis}
\label{sec:errors}
The CNN made 726 errors out of 10,000 test images (error rate 7.26%), fewer than every classical configuration. The most frequent CNN confusion was Shirt predicted as T-shirt/top (99 images, 9.9\% of true Shirts), followed by T-shirt/top predicted as Shirt (65, 6.5\%), Shirt predicted as Coat (62, 6.2\%), and Shirt predicted as Pullover (60, 6.0\%). These errors show that visually similar upper-body garments remain difficult at $28\times28$ resolution. The CNN made 321 fewer errors than the pixel RBF-SVM, which made 1,047 errors. McNemar tests comparing the CNN with each classical model were significant after Bonferroni correction (all adjusted $p$-values were below $2.0\times10^{-28}$). This indicates paired prediction differences on this test set, not variation across training seeds.

\section{Discussion, Limitations and Future Improvements}
\label{sec:discussion}
The CNN outperformed all eight classical configurations on the held-out test set, reaching 92.74\% accuracy and 0.9269 macro-F1. The strongest classical result was the pixel RBF-SVM at 89.53\% accuracy and 0.8951 macro-F1. The CNN also had the lowest observed error count. The paired McNemar comparisons were significant after correcting for eight comparisons. Inference timings suggest a trade-off: the CNN's recorded GPU tensor latency was 0.0116 ms/image, but that excludes preprocessing; classical end-to-end timings include feature extraction and should not be compared to that figure directly.

Several limitations qualify these findings. The evaluation covers one benchmark dataset, one split and one random seed; it does not establish performance on real inventory photographs or quantify training-seed variability. The CNN hyperparameters were not tuned, while the classical models used small grids. The pixel logistic-regression fit reached the 500-iteration cap with a convergence warning. The HOG RBF-SVM used only 11,770 of 54,000 training images because its estimated full fit exceeded the 30-minute runtime gate, so its score is not directly comparable to full-data fits. Inference timings were recorded as single timed passes after warm-up and should be repeated for robust latency estimates. Future work should validate on real inventory images, repeat training across seeds, expand hyperparameter searches within a documented budget, investigate logistic-regression convergence, and benchmark all models with repeated runs under matched preprocessing and hardware conditions.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
For this Fashion-MNIST benchmark, the small CNN was the strongest evaluated model, with 0.9274 test accuracy and 0.9269 macro-F1. Among classical models, the pixel RBF-SVM performed best (0.8953 accuracy, 0.8951 macro-F1). The selected HOG representation improved validation macro-F1 over the original HOG baseline, but the runtime-gated HOG SVM fit and other protocol limitations should be considered when interpreting the comparison. The results support the CNN for this benchmark, not yet for production inventory tagging without further real-world validation.
